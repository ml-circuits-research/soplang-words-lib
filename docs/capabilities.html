What the library can do

By job, not by API. Each job has a paragraph, an example that runs (every example on this page is run in CI and must print what is shown), the module and the version it came in, and its limits. Where a job is not done yet, it says so. API details: api, reference; how worlds combine: patterns.

Three kinds of worlds are named throughout (see the glossary): a world schema says what a kind of world is, a knowledge world holds facts true for everyone, and a live world is one running instance of a schema in a session, reading knowledge worlds. Each job below says which kind it works on; what each kind may contain and where code lives is on kinds of worlds.

Describe a world

In core: core.type, core.slot; since 0.1. Works on: a world schema (its types and slots).

Kinds of things are types; their attributes are slots with kinds, value lists, ranges, defaults and questions to ask when they are missing. Everything told is checked against them at once. Limits: one value per key unless the slot says many; a missing required key is an open question, not a refusal.

State facts and rules

In core: core.rule, core.deny; since 0.1. Works on: any world: a world schema's rules, a knowledge world's facts, a live world's state.

Rules derive wires from wires; a conclusion goes when what it rests on goes. Denies refuse a change that breaks them; soft denies only advise. Limits: no rule may negate or aggregate what its own conclusions produce (stratified).

A rule joins two things through a reference by naming the other pattern (@r line order=$o qty=?q with @o order ...): a variable (order=?x) would join nothing.

Ask and explain

In world.query, world.why; since 0.1. Works on: any world; why on a live world.

query answers with a status (yes, no, unknown, supported, ...) and what the answer rests on; why gives the chain behind a wire or a value, back to what was told (the rule, the act, the tier, the source).

Act, undo, groups

In world.act, world.undo; since 0.5. Works on: a live world.

Actions change the world in one transaction (declared in SOP Lang, or a host function); several requests of one sentence go together or not at all and are one undo step; every action returns its difference. Limits: a collection replaced with world.sync is not given back by undo (take a mark()).

Worlds that do things, as data; refuse with alternatives

In std.schema, std.acts; since 0.18. Works on: a world schema (std.schema) and the live world it runs (std.acts).

A world is a schema (std.schema): kinds, attributes, which acts apply. The acts (std.acts: make, remove, change, place, connect, group, pick, ask, copy, send, save, load, undo, redo, show, teach, forget) are the same everywhere; a new world needs no rule. A refusal says what is allowed and where. Limits: place writes a position key; relations are std.relations' job.

Drive a world whose state is not wires

In std.schema.effect; since 0.28. Works on: a live world whose state is partly the host's.

One act on one kind can be given to the host (a drawing board, a diagram engine, a face): the act still checks the schema, its result and difference come back like any act's, and a refused sentence undoes the effects done before the refusal. Limits: world.undo of an effect act takes back its wires only; the host keeps its own history.

Fill gaps from other worlds

In std.fill, std.know; since 0.17. Works on: a live world, filled from knowledge worlds.

When a request leaves a key out, other modules offer values in named tiers (std.fill: user, focus, scene, taught, knowledge, default); the best tier wins, the source is kept, a tie is advice. std.know offers from a knowledge catalog.

Keep focus in a dialogue

In std.focus, std.dialogue; since 0.17. Works on: a live world.

std.focus keeps what a dialogue is about after each turn (thing, action, place, world); a request that leaves out its thing or action is completed from the turn before, and earlier turns keep theirs. std.dialogue (0.47) gives each turn its kind (why, correction, continuation, list, ellipsis, answer, request) from the host's cue phrases, with dialogueTurn and focusLog. Limits: a value chosen by std.fill is not a mention.

Relate and place things

In std.relations, std.boxes, placeFor, boxesOf, relationsOf; since 0.16. Works on: a live world.

std.relations: on, in, next to, above, in front of, kept: when the other thing moves, the place follows; rows; the first; one per place. std.boxes has the geometry functions. placeFor (0.62) gives the spot for one thing (next to another, in a region, a default spot) avoiding overlaps; relation kinds are declared with std.schema.relation. placeFor (0.62) gives the spot for one thing (next to another, in a region, a default spot) avoiding overlaps; relation kinds are declared with std.schema.relation. Limits: boxes only; a row assumes equal widths.

Share things between views; teach facts

In std.schema views, show, teach, complete; since 0.21. Works on: a live world and its views; taught facts join it at the tier taught.

Several views read the same things; a view shows some aspects; showing a thing that lacks one is refused with the reason. Facts the user teaches sit at their own tier and say who taught them; the act complete applies defaults learnt later to things already made. Limits: one value per key for all views (a view restyles in its renderer).

Generate cards, examples, replies, a coverage grid and tests

In schemaCard, schemaExamples, actReply, schemaGrid, gridTests; since 0.19. Works on: a world schema (cards, examples, grid, tests); a live world (replies).

From a schema: the card of a world (kinds, attributes, acts, words), example sentences per act with their requests, the plain-English reply for a done act or a refusal, a coverage grid (every world × kind × act case run, with its expected difference; the holes are the cases not done) and a node:test file from the grid. Limits: English only (hosts translate).

Drop readings the present state would refuse

In world.score; since 0.13. Works on: a live world.

score judges candidate readings of one sentence without applying anything: which would be refused and why, which lack something and what to ask.

Measure reuse

In tools/reuse.mjs, option advise; since 0.21. Works on: world schemas.

tools/reuse.mjs finds candidates for shared modules across worlds (types and rules of the same shape, shared words, parameterised worlds) and a reuse number per world; the option advise gives the same advice to an author at load.

Load knowledge by lookup

In createCatalog, bundleReader, bundleThenRead, listOf, loadBundles; since 0.23. Works on: knowledge worlds, loaded into a live world.

A catalog (an index of worlds with their schemas, dependencies and shards, and a vocabulary from words to shards) finds what a word needs, reads each shard once through the host's read (a file, fetch, a bundle), caches it, and tells it into a world with the schemas first. tools/build-bundle.mjs writes an offline bundle for a list of words that bundleReader reads with the same code. bundleThenRead (0.49) answers from a bundled subset first and the catalog after, with reachable(); listOf lists loaded facts of a type; loadBundles tells a world's declared bundle once. Limits: the lemma of a word is the host's (lemma option); the library knows no language.

Handle one request end to end

In turn; since 0.24. Works on: a live world.

turn takes the candidate readings of one message, tries each on a fork, applies the first the world takes as one sentence, and gives back the difference, the why of each changed value, the guesses it made and the replies; when none is taken, a graded fallback: one question, "not here but there", or the best guess named. The host reads text into readings and renders. Limits: the readings are the host's; replies are plain English unless the host passes reply.

Change the path of a request by telling facts

In std.turn, turnFlow; since 0.26. Works on: a live world (its pipeline is a world schema of its own, std.turn).

The stages of turn are declared with std.turn: an application adds a stage (a confirmation before removing many things, a permission check), gives a stage a second provider ranked by tier (a second reader), or skips one, by telling wires; turnFlow runs the declared path and gives a trace of every stage taken. Limits: what a stage does is a function; candidates are tried one after the other, not in parallel.

Choose a world for a request

In route, arbitrate; since 0.24. Works on: world schemas (which one a request is for).

route scores the worlds of the schemas for a request by their cards, the thing it names, a dry run, and a signal of the host's own (an encoder's score), best first with the reasons. arbitrate (0.45) gives each world a verdict instead: own, decline (it has no use for the kind: the library's rule) or refuse (it has the kind but not the act, or its dry run refuses; only in the exact pass), with a resolves hook for ties. Limits: no reading of text: the request comes from the host.

Tools for authors

In tools/validate.mjs, tools/ledger.mjs, ledgerGroups; since 0.25. Works on: world schemas and knowledge worlds.

Validate a world in one command (what is refused, what is noted, the grid's holes); keep a failure ledger (append-only, grouped by the pipeline stage where a case failed and by cause signals the host plugs in); find reuse; measure with the benches in bench/. Limits: the cause signals and the replay of the sources are the host's.

Read noun phrases with a language's knowledge world

In parseText, nounPhrase; since 0.29. Works on: a knowledge world about a language (the grammar); resolve then reads a live world.

A language is a world of facts (its lexicon, its rules, its coordinating words); the library's parser reads text with it into noun phrases with determiners, numbers, modifiers and a prepositional phrase, and coordinations, every parse best first. Limits: noun phrases only for now (verbs and frames next); the grammar of a real language lives in sop-knowledge-worlds, not here.

Resolve what a phrase refers to

In resolve; since 0.31. Works on: a live world, with a grammar (a knowledge world about a language).

The things a noun phrase names, and how sure the reading is (user, scene, focus, slip), with each step in how. It handles synonyms through core.word, modifiers, names, ordinals, numbers, sets, coordination (a choice is asked), relations, pronouns and the focus, "the other one", and parts of a set. Limits: time phrases wait for an order in time; the words come from the grammar and core.word, so there is no language here.

Fill any key from a catalog

In std.know (std.know.fill); since 0.35. Works on: a live world, filled from knowledge worlds.

One line says which keys a catalog type gives when make leaves them out: std.know.fill from=<type> match=<key> key=<key> .... The value comes at tier knowledge with its source, and a choice at a better tier wins. Limits: only on make; complete applies what std.fill chose later.

Say what must be loaded first

In std.schema.needs, needsOf, turn({ load }); since 0.39. Works on: a live world and the world schemas or views it follows.

A world or a view says which types it needs loaded before acting. An act there is refused with the list until they are; turn asks the host to load them and dry-runs again. Limits: "loaded" means some wire of the type exists; what to load is the host's.

Act on a view itself

In std.schema.view state=; since 0.37. Works on: a live world's view.

A view's own attributes (zoom, the layer shown) live on one thing of a declared kind, made on first use with its defaults. "Zoom in" names no thing: the view whose state has the attribute is meant. Limits: one state thing per view.

Ask over attributes and relations

In std.acts (ask); since 0.38. Works on: a live world and the knowledge worlds loaded in it.

ask answers with the shape of the answer: a number with its unit, a value, a list, or yes / no. It takes a thing or a knowledge fact by name, a relation either way round, "which" and "how many" of a kind. Limits: one relation per question; no chains.

Read several knowledge repositories

In createCatalogs; since 0.43. Works on: knowledge worlds from several repositories, loaded into a live world.

Several catalogs in an order of precedence. Each fact keeps its catalog in its source, and a thing two catalogs define differently is reported while the first version stays. Limits: a conflict is found by id, or by type and name.

Cut a message into clauses

In segment, actWords; since 0.46. Works on: a language's knowledge world (its joiners and frame leads) and the live world's acts.

One segmenter that knows the act forms: a joiner cuts where an act word follows it, lists and coordinations stay whole, a run of joiners is one, and a comma after a frame lead stays. Hosts need no clause encoder. Limits: "it" in the second clause is resolved later (by resolve and the focus), not here; corrections ("no wait") are std.dialogue's.

Inflect and analyse the words of any language

In morphOf, generate, analyse, fold, splitClitics, dictionary, realise, templateFor; since 0.52. Works on: a language's knowledge world (its paradigm classes, folds and templates) and its external dictionary.

A language world is compact rules plus a dictionary: paradigm classes (a stem rule and the endings per feature cell, inheritance with overrides, alternations), irregulars, spelling folds, clitic joiners and realisation templates. The library generates and analyses forms with the same declarations (a suffix index, no form lists) and realises a template with agreement. Limits: one suffix per cell (no prefixes or infixes yet); syntax-level agreement in the parser is still to come; w.lang.ro is not published yet, so the Romanian measurements wait.

Change states over time by rules

In motionPlan, motionRun, readMotion, planMotionFacts, splitText; since 0.54. Works on: a live world's view (a face, a drawing, a diagram) and the rules a world schema gives it.

Rules as data: on an event (with a condition on its fields), or repeated idly, a sequence of steps sets keys of a state for durations. Priorities hold a key, and a new event cancels the rest of the earlier one. A step can play one state per unit of a stream (the generic form of lip sync). The plan is pure and seeded; the host plays it. Since 0.56 the library also reads the knowledge repository's full format (k.motion: layers, during, until, emits, holds until an event, conditions on features, via, splitters as facts). Limits: the steps of one rule run in sequence (no parallel branches inside a rule); durations are whole milliseconds.

Reach a goal by methods that are knowledge

In planner, factStore, PlanLive, readMethods, PLAN_LIMITS; since 0.55. Works on: a knowledge world of methods (k.procedures) and a live world.

Complex and planned actions come from knowledge and orchestration, not from code in live worlds. A goal ("a person sitting on a swing", "set a table for four") is reached by the methods of a knowledge world. The planner chooses among alternatives by fit, world, preconditions and rank. It decomposes steps into further methods, fills roles from the goal, earlier steps, the live world, history and knowledge, and explains each step in one line. Limits: depth 3, 200 acts. The requests are named by k.actions roles: the table that maps them onto std.acts arguments (Q44 a) and a one-call apply come next, so for now the host applies requests with that mapping.

Suggest random commands that work

In sample, diversity; since 0.59. Works on: a live world, its world schemas and the knowledge they join.

Randomness as a mechanism, with no example lists: small commands chosen from what the worlds allow in the current state, with values legal for their slots and kinds from the catalogs the worlds join. Recency and novelty weigh in, it is seeded, and every suggestion is checked by a dry run (and, with the host's language world, by reading it back). Limits: place is not suggested (it needs a space the host knows); the sentences come from the host's realiser until the generation templates of Round 30 land.

Summarise a log or a text

In actsOf, summarizeLog, summarizeText, overlap; since 0.61. Works on: a live world's history; any text.

A history of acts becomes a few frames: acts and kinds counted, siblings generalised up the chain of kinds, failures kept, the most salient first, for the language world to render. A text gets an extractive summary by centrality, information and position, with nothing said twice and every sentence from the source. Limits: text summaries are extractive; regenerating sentences from frames waits for the language worlds' generation; the reference set is small and written for the tests.

Compose a text from knowledge

In compose, discourseRealiser, discourseTemplates, inflectBy, articleFor, writeDocument, readDocument, solveChoices, readStory, storyPlan, STORY_RELATIONS, interestOf, measuresOf, shapeShare, interestModel; since 0.64. Works on: knowledge worlds (facts about a topic, genres, interest features).

A composition with the moves of a genre: an outline from the topic's relations across the worlds, chosen by interest (the published k.interest: appraisals and features over measures computed from facts, each score explained), facts that each cite their wire and never repeat, and an exact size or a list of what the knowledge lacked. With the published k.genres, the layout follows the genre's parts, steps and register, and its choice points are solved jointly. A story is planned from k.story: its elements are solved jointly under its constraints over the catalog. Limits: measures of words and sentences (word band, concreteness, words over the register's maximum) come from the host. The sentences come from the language world's discourse templates when it has them (the clause of a fact is the host's). A composition is written as a w.document live world (writeDocument) and read back in order (readDocument). The procedures of k.procedures come next. The sentences come from the host's realiser.

Read a question by the types of its words

In readByTypes, typedLexicon, nearWords, normalise, alternationsOf, wordClassesOf, canonicalise, readCanonical, fillersFrom, storyState; since 0.72. Works on: any world with facts (names, keys, values) and acts.

"what sound does a cow make", "a cow makes which sound" and "cow sound?" read to one frame, the attribute sound of the thing cow, because the words are typed through the world's own facts and order only ranks. Inverse questions ("which animal says moo") and acts with arguments by type are read too. An unknown word is read as its nearest known word through k.words senses (a synonym, a kind), with a cost. Variants (passive, "could you", "please", dative shift) are brought back to the canonical form by the language world's alternation rules, written once. readCanonical puts the inverse operators in front of the reader: clauses split, fillers dropped, alternations undone. A question after a word problem's story is answered from the story's quantities (storyState). Limits: a first version. The alternation rules are a toy set until the language world publishes them; induced rules are the next item (questions.md Q54).

Judge a fix on the cousins of its case

In cousins, ancestorsOf, kindChain, judgeFix, learnRules, learnFormOperators, formOperator, formEdits, learnFormEdits, familyOf, liftTokens; since 0.73. Works on: worlds with kinds (kind, class, is, is_a) and facts with keys.

A failing case is expanded into its siblings and cousins (up the kinds, then down to other members), valid ones only. A fix is then judged on the case and on its cousins by degree, which measures how general it is. learnRules induces reading rules from cases (the library's induce, fillers lifted to their kind) and admits them only on held-out cases and cousins. learnFormOperators extracts the few operators of form (wrappers, fillers, passive) from aligned pairs and counts how many cover 80 % and 95 % of them. Each is a cousin generator and a normalisation rule. Limits: the dimensions are fillers and keys. The others (act families, alternations, world structure kinds) come with the items of Q54. A cousin's wording is the case's text with the filler replaced, until generation is used.

Align two worlds by structure

In alignWorlds; since 0.77. Works on: any two world schemas.

A seating world and a timeline world align: table to day, seat to slot, guest to event. Types are paired by the shape of their slots and references, not by their names. What one has and the other lacks (a default, a closed set of choices, a required slot) is offered with the map, never told. Limits: the offers are defaults, choices and required slots. Rules, procedures and question forms come next. The pairing is greedy (the best pairs first).

Measure what a mechanism buys

In experiment, ablation; since 0.96. Works on: any host function and a set of cells with expectations.

Cells with expectations become ledger rows. An ablation runs every configuration on every set and reports a table, the cells each mechanism gains or loses against the baseline, and the failures grouped by cause. Limits: the host's run and judge decide what a pass is. The rows are in memory: a host appends them to its ledger with ledgerAppend.

Learn worlds from use (agent skills)

In skills/; since 0.97. Works on: an application, its failures and held-out rows, and the knowledge repositories in repos.pin.

Six skills turn the library's tools into steps an agent follows. They learn a world from examples, improve it from failures, learn language rules, consolidate, audit knowledge, and expand and test a case. Each has a dry run on real material, listed in skills/README.md. Limits: the dry runs read psys2's form pairs and the pinned catalog. A host's own run needs its adapter: the reader and the answers.
