## 2. The new gaps, with a proposal for each

| Gap | What is missing, and what I propose |
|---|---|
| M. Placing a group by a pattern | A pattern of placement is a set of positions generated from a region of the shape space (the vertices of a hexagon, points along a line or a circle, the cells of a grid, the outline of a house) plus an assignment of the members to the positions (by order, or keeping each as near as possible to where it is). One member may be excepted and given its own place ("with the circle in the middle"). It is an operation on a whole (6.7), next to "arrange better". The same operation: trees in a row, sections in two columns, meetings spread evenly over the week. |
| N. Which order "first" and "last" mean | Each kind of whole declares its orders, with one default: flow order for a flowchart, reading order (left to right, top to bottom) for a page, time for a calendar, text order for a document. The reply names the order when it was not the only one possible. |
| O. Referring to the past | The journal of 6.11 already holds, for every step, the values before and after. So "where it was", "as before", "the old title", "back to Monday" can be resolved: the value this quality or relation of this thing had before the last step that changed it (or before the step the user points at). Restoring it is an ordinary change with that value, and an ordinary step. It is not undo: other things keep their later changes. |
| P. Questions about the world | A third kind of question beside state and capabilities: answered from the knowledge bases when they hold the fact, with the source; otherwise "I do not know that". Never invented. This is the same mechanism as explaining (6.11) applied to knowledge instead of to a step. |
| Q. A reaction that names no dimension | "Too much", "you overdid it", "calm it down": applies to the result of the last step, on the dimension where that result is farthest above what is usual for its kind (the count of things, the sizes, the saturation of colours). If two dimensions are about equally far, ask. The reply names the dimension chosen. |
| R. Referring by where a thing came from | Every value and every thing has its source and character (6.6). So "the extra ones", "the ones you added", "what I asked for", "your guess" are references by provenance: things whose existence is an assumption of the system, respectively a requirement of the user. One more row in the table of ways to refer (6.4). |
| S. A definite that points at an expected part | Extend R37. A definite reference that finds nothing may still authorise making the thing when it names a part that the graph or the knowledge of a thing in focus expects: "the fish" after a pond, "the roof" after a house, "the signature" after a letter, "the agenda" after a meeting. The reply says it was added. Otherwise R37 stands. |
| T. Structure defined by the user | The user may declare named regions of a space in the instance ("three levels: water, land, sky"; "two columns"; "morning for deep work"). They are region facts of the instance with the user as source; they can be referred to by name and by order; and "distribute logically" assigns each thing to the region whose name or kind knowledge links it to (a fish to water), which is gap F's objective with the user's regions as the places. |
| U. Learning what is usual | Generalise 4.5.1 from degree factors to usual values. When the user rejects a result and states what he expected for a kind ("a diagram: 5–10 nodes, arrows"), or repeatedly corrects the same completed value in the same direction, the usual region for that kind in this kind of world moves, as a learnt fact with provenance, undone with its step. Same episode rule as R33. |
| V. A topic with structure | The topic of a text is a small graph of the same sort as a request: kinds, a place, characters, what they do, and possibly another topic inside. `compose` receives that graph, and the knowledge statements are gathered for each kind in it (lake, narrator, frog), not for one string. |
