What the library can do

By job, not by API. Each job has a paragraph, an example that runs (every example on this page is run in CI and must print what is shown), the module and the version it came in, and its limits. Where a job is not done yet, it says so. API details: api, reference; how worlds combine: patterns.

Three kinds of worlds are named throughout (see the glossary): a world schema says what a kind of world is, a knowledge world holds facts true for everyone, and a live world is one running instance of a schema in a session, reading knowledge worlds. Each job below says which kind it works on; what each kind may contain and where code lives is on kinds of worlds.

Describe a world

In core: core.type, core.slot; since 0.1. Works on: a world schema (its types and slots).

Kinds of things are types; their attributes are slots with kinds, value lists, ranges, defaults and questions to ask when they are missing. Everything told is checked against them at once. Limits: one value per key unless the slot says many; a missing required key is an open question, not a refusal.

State facts and rules

In core: core.rule, core.deny; since 0.1. Works on: any world: a world schema's rules, a knowledge world's facts, a live world's state.

Rules derive wires from wires; a conclusion goes when what it rests on goes. Denies refuse a change that breaks them; soft denies only advise. Limits: no rule may negate or aggregate what its own conclusions produce (stratified).

A rule joins two things through a reference by naming the other pattern (@r line order=$o qty=?q with @o order ...): a variable (order=?x) would join nothing.

Ask and explain

In world.query, world.why; since 0.1. Works on: any world; why on a live world.

query answers with a status (yes, no, unknown, supported, ...) and what the answer rests on; why gives the chain behind a wire or a value, back to what was told (the rule, the act, the tier, the source).

Act, undo, groups

In world.act, world.undo; since 0.5. Works on: a live world.

Actions change the world in one transaction (declared in SOP Lang, or a host function); several requests of one sentence go together or not at all and are one undo step; every action returns its difference. Limits: a collection replaced with world.sync is not given back by undo (take a mark()).

Worlds that do things, as data; refuse with alternatives

In std.schema, std.acts; since 0.18. Works on: a world schema (std.schema) and the live world it runs (std.acts).

A world is a schema (std.schema): kinds, attributes, which acts apply. The acts (std.acts: make, remove, change, place, connect, group, pick, ask, copy, send, save, load, undo, redo, show, teach, forget) are the same everywhere; a new world needs no rule. A refusal says what is allowed and where. Limits: place writes a position key; relations are std.relations' job.

Drive a world whose state is not wires

In std.schema.effect; since 0.28. Works on: a live world whose state is partly the host's.

One act on one kind can be given to the host (a drawing board, a diagram engine, a face): the act still checks the schema, its result and difference come back like any act's, and a refused sentence undoes the effects done before the refusal. Limits: world.undo of an effect act takes back its wires only; the host keeps its own history.

Fill gaps from other worlds

In std.fill, std.know; since 0.17. Works on: a live world, filled from knowledge worlds.

When a request leaves a key out, other modules offer values in named tiers (std.fill: user, focus, scene, taught, knowledge, default); the best tier wins, the source is kept, a tie is advice. std.know offers from a knowledge catalog.

Keep focus in a dialogue

In std.focus, std.dialogue; since 0.17. Works on: a live world.

std.focus keeps what a dialogue is about after each turn (thing, action, place, world); a request that leaves out its thing or action is completed from the turn before, and earlier turns keep theirs. std.dialogue (0.47) gives each turn its kind (why, correction, continuation, list, ellipsis, answer, request) from the host's cue phrases, with dialogueTurn and focusLog. Limits: a value chosen by std.fill is not a mention.

Relate and place things

In std.relations, std.boxes, placeFor, arrange, outlineOf, boxesOf, relationsOf; since 0.16. Works on: a live world.

std.relations: on, in, next to, above, in front of, kept: when the other thing moves, the place follows; rows; the first; one per place. std.boxes has the geometry functions. placeFor (0.62) gives the spot for one thing (next to another, in a region, a default spot) avoiding overlaps; relation kinds are declared with std.schema.relation. placeFor (0.62) gives the spot for one thing (next to another, in a region, a default spot) avoiding overlaps; relation kinds are declared with std.schema.relation. Limits: boxes only; a row assumes equal widths.

Share things between views; teach facts

In std.schema views, show, teach, complete; since 0.21. Works on: a live world and its views; taught facts join it at the tier taught.

Several views read the same things; a view shows some aspects; showing a thing that lacks one is refused with the reason. Facts the user teaches sit at their own tier and say who taught them; the act complete applies defaults learnt later to things already made. Limits: one value per key for all views (a view restyles in its renderer).

Generate cards, examples, replies, a coverage grid and tests

In schemaCard, schemaExamples, actReply, schemaGrid, gridTests; since 0.19. Works on: a world schema (cards, examples, grid, tests); a live world (replies).

From a schema: the card of a world (kinds, attributes, acts, words), example sentences per act with their requests, the plain-English reply for a done act or a refusal, a coverage grid (every world × kind × act case run, with its expected difference; the holes are the cases not done) and a node:test file from the grid. Limits: English only (hosts translate).

Drop readings the present state would refuse

In world.score; since 0.13. Works on: a live world.

score judges candidate readings of one sentence without applying anything: which would be refused and why, which lack something and what to ask.

Measure reuse

In tools/reuse.mjs, option advise; since 0.21. Works on: world schemas.

tools/reuse.mjs finds candidates for shared modules across worlds (types and rules of the same shape, shared words, parameterised worlds) and a reuse number per world; the option advise gives the same advice to an author at load.

Load knowledge by lookup

In createCatalog, bundleReader, bundleThenRead, listOf, loadBundles; since 0.23. Works on: knowledge worlds, loaded into a live world.

A catalog (an index of worlds with their schemas, dependencies and shards, and a vocabulary from words to shards) finds what a word needs, reads each shard once through the host's read (a file, fetch, a bundle), caches it, and tells it into a world with the schemas first. tools/build-bundle.mjs writes an offline bundle for a list of words that bundleReader reads with the same code. bundleThenRead (0.49) answers from a bundled subset first and the catalog after, with reachable(); listOf lists loaded facts of a type; loadBundles tells a world's declared bundle once. Limits: the lemma of a word is the host's (lemma option); the library knows no language.

Handle one request end to end

In turn; since 0.24. Works on: a live world.

turn takes the candidate readings of one message, tries each on a fork, applies the first the world takes as one sentence, and gives back the difference, the why of each changed value, the guesses it made and the replies; when none is taken, a graded fallback: one question, "not here but there", or the best guess named. The host reads text into readings and renders. Limits: the readings are the host's; replies are plain English unless the host passes reply.

Change the path of a request by telling facts

In std.turn, turnFlow, TURN_STAGES; since 0.26. Works on: a live world (its pipeline is a world schema of its own, std.turn).

The stages of turn are declared with std.turn: an application adds a stage (a confirmation before removing many things, a permission check), gives a stage a second provider ranked by tier (a second reader), or skips one, by telling wires; turnFlow runs the declared path and gives a trace of every stage taken. Limits: what a stage does is a function; candidates are tried one after the other, not in parallel.

Choose a world for a request

In arbitrate; since 0.24. Works on: world schemas (which one a request is for).

arbitrate gives each world a verdict for a request: own, decline (it has no use for the kind: the library's rule) or refuse (it has the kind but not the act, or its dry run refuses; only in the exact pass), with a resolves hook for ties. Among several owners, std.decide kind world chooses: the coverage of the request's words, then the world's priority; an application's own criterion (the open tab) is the host's. Limits: no reading of text: the request comes from the host.

Tools for authors

In tools/validate.mjs, tools/ledger.mjs, ledgerGroups; since 0.25. Works on: world schemas and knowledge worlds.

Validate a world in one command (what is refused, what is noted, the grid's holes); keep a failure ledger (append-only, grouped by the pipeline stage where a case failed and by cause signals the host plugs in); find reuse; measure with the benches in bench/. Limits: the cause signals and the replay of the sources are the host's.

Read noun phrases with a language's knowledge world

In parseText, nounPhrase; since 0.29. Works on: a knowledge world about a language (the grammar); resolve then reads a live world.

A language is a world of facts (its lexicon, its rules, its coordinating words); the library's parser reads text with it into noun phrases with determiners, numbers, modifiers and a prepositional phrase, and coordinations, every parse best first. Limits: noun phrases only for now (verbs and frames next); the grammar of a real language lives in sop-knowledge-worlds, not here.

Resolve what a phrase refers to

In resolve; since 0.31. Works on: a live world, with a grammar (a knowledge world about a language).

The things a noun phrase names, and how sure the reading is (user, scene, focus, slip), with each step in how. It handles synonyms through core.word, modifiers, names, ordinals, numbers, sets, coordination (a choice is asked), relations, pronouns and the focus, "the other one", and parts of a set. Limits: time phrases wait for an order in time; the words come from the grammar and core.word, so there is no language here.

Fill any key from a catalog

In std.know (std.know.fill); since 0.35. Works on: a live world, filled from knowledge worlds.

One line says which keys a catalog type gives when make leaves them out: std.know.fill from=<type> match=<key> key=<key> .... The value comes at tier knowledge with its source, and a choice at a better tier wins. Limits: only on make; complete applies what std.fill chose later.

Say what must be loaded first

In std.schema.needs, needsOf, turn({ load }); since 0.39. Works on: a live world and the world schemas or views it follows.

A world or a view says which types it needs loaded before acting. An act there is refused with the list until they are; turn asks the host to load them and dry-runs again. Limits: "loaded" means some wire of the type exists; what to load is the host's.

Act on a view itself

In std.schema.view state=; since 0.37. Works on: a live world's view.

A view's own attributes (zoom, the layer shown) live on one thing of a declared kind, made on first use with its defaults. "Zoom in" names no thing: the view whose state has the attribute is meant. Limits: one state thing per view.

Ask over attributes and relations

In std.acts (ask); since 0.38. Works on: a live world and the knowledge worlds loaded in it.

ask answers with the shape of the answer: a number with its unit, a value, a list, or yes / no. It takes a thing or a knowledge fact by name, a relation either way round, "which" and "how many" of a kind. Limits: one relation per question; no chains.

Read several knowledge repositories

In createCatalogs; since 0.43. Works on: knowledge worlds from several repositories, loaded into a live world.

Several catalogs in an order of precedence. Each fact keeps its catalog in its source, and a thing two catalogs define differently is reported while the first version stays. Limits: a conflict is found by id, or by type and name.

Cut a message into clauses

In segment, actWords; since 0.46. Works on: a language's knowledge world (its joiners and frame leads) and the live world's acts.

One segmenter that knows the act forms: a joiner cuts where an act word follows it, lists and coordinations stay whole, a run of joiners is one, and a comma after a frame lead stays. Hosts need no clause encoder. Limits: "it" in the second clause is resolved later (by resolve and the focus), not here; corrections ("no wait") are std.dialogue's.

Inflect and analyse the words of any language

In morphOf, generate, analyse, fold, splitClitics, dictionary, realise, templateFor; since 0.52. Works on: a language's knowledge world (its paradigm classes, folds and templates) and its external dictionary.

A language world is compact rules plus a dictionary: paradigm classes (a stem rule and the endings per feature cell, inheritance with overrides, alternations), irregulars, spelling folds, clitic joiners and realisation templates. The library generates and analyses forms with the same declarations (a suffix index, no form lists) and realises a template with agreement. Limits: one suffix per cell (no prefixes or infixes yet); syntax-level agreement in the parser is still to come; w.lang.ro is not published yet, so the Romanian measurements wait.

Change states over time by rules

In readMotion, planMotionFacts, splitText, motionRun; since 0.54. Works on: a live world's view (a face, a drawing, a diagram) and the rules a world schema gives it.

Rules as data: on an event (with a condition on its fields), or repeated idly, a sequence of steps sets keys of a state for durations. Priorities hold a key, and a new event cancels the rest of the earlier one. A step can play one state per unit of a stream (the generic form of lip sync). The plan is pure and seeded; the host plays it. The rules are the knowledge repository's format (k.motion: layers, during, until, emits, holds until an event, conditions on features, via, splitters as facts). Limits: the steps of one rule run in sequence (no parallel branches inside a rule); durations are whole milliseconds.

Reach a goal by methods that are knowledge

In planner, factStore, PlanLive, readMethods, PLAN_LIMITS; since 0.55. Works on: a knowledge world of methods (k.procedures) and a live world.

Complex and planned actions come from knowledge and orchestration, not from code in live worlds. A goal ("a person sitting on a swing", "set a table for four") is reached by the methods of a knowledge world. The planner chooses among alternatives by fit, world, preconditions and rank. It decomposes steps into further methods, fills roles from the goal, earlier steps, the live world, history and knowledge, and explains each step in one line. Limits: depth 3, 200 acts. The requests are named by k.actions roles: the table that maps them onto std.acts arguments (Q44 a) and a one-call apply come next, so for now the host applies requests with that mapping.

Suggest random commands that work

In sample, diversity; since 0.59. Works on: a live world, its world schemas and the knowledge they join.

Randomness as a mechanism, with no example lists: small commands chosen from what the worlds allow in the current state, with values legal for their slots and kinds from the catalogs the worlds join. Recency and novelty weigh in, it is seeded, and every suggestion is checked by a dry run (and, with the host's language world, by reading it back). Limits: place is not suggested (it needs a space the host knows); the sentences come from the host's realiser until the generation templates of Round 30 land.

Summarise a log or a text

In actsOf, summarizeLog, summarizeText, overlap; since 0.61. Works on: a live world's history; any text.

A history of acts becomes a few frames: acts and kinds counted, siblings generalised up the chain of kinds, failures kept, the most salient first, for the language world to render. A text gets an extractive summary by centrality, information and position, with nothing said twice and every sentence from the source. Limits: text summaries are extractive; regenerating sentences from frames waits for the language worlds' generation; the reference set is small and written for the tests.

Compose a text from knowledge

In compose, discourseRealiser, discourseTemplates, joinList, clauseOf, phraseOf, narrationOf, realiseFrame, paraphrasesOf, actSentence, renderFrames, inflectBy, articleFor, writeDocument, readDocument, solveChoices, readStory, storyPlan, STORY_RELATIONS, interestOf, measuresOf, shapeShare, interestModel; since 0.64. Works on: knowledge worlds (facts about a topic, genres, interest features).

A composition with the moves of a genre: an outline from the topic's relations across the worlds, chosen by interest (the published k.interest: appraisals and features over measures computed from facts, each score explained), facts that each cite their wire and never repeat, and an exact size or a list of what the knowledge lacked. With the published k.genres, the layout follows the genre's parts, steps and register, and its choice points are solved jointly. A story is planned from k.story: its elements are solved jointly under its constraints over the catalog. Limits: measures of words and sentences (word band, concreteness, words over the register's maximum) come from the host. The sentences come from the language world's discourse templates when it has them (the clause of a fact is the host's). A composition is written as a w.document live world (writeDocument) and read back in order (readDocument). The procedures of k.procedures come next. The sentences come from the host's realiser.

Read a question by the types of its words

In readText, moodOf, confidentTypo, READERS, readByTypes, typedLexicon, nearWords, normalise, alternationsOf, wordClassesOf, canonicalise, readCanonical, splitRunOn, fillersFrom, storyState, readEnsemble, defaultReaders, riskCoverage, calibrate, signatureOf, chunk, minePatterns, patternReader, resolveEllipsis; since 0.72. Works on: any world with facts (names, keys, values) and acts.

"what sound does a cow make", "a cow makes which sound" and "cow sound?" read to one frame, the attribute sound of the thing cow, because the words are typed through the world's own facts and order only ranks. Inverse questions ("which animal says moo") and acts with arguments by type are read too. An unknown word is read as its nearest known word through k.words senses (a synonym, a kind), with a cost. Variants (passive, "could you", "please", dative shift) are brought back to the canonical form by the language world's alternation rules, written once. readCanonical puts the inverse operators in front of the reader: clauses split, fillers dropped, alternations undone. readEnsemble runs several readers as voices and returns a frame with a confidence, or a question back when they disagree. A question after a word problem's story is answered from the story's quantities (storyState). Limits: a first version. The alternation rules are a toy set until the language world publishes them; induced rules are the next item (questions.md Q54).

Judge a fix on the related cases of its case

In relatedCases, ancestorsOf, kindChain, judgeOnRelated, learnRules, learnFormOperators, formOperator, formEdits, learnFormEdits, familyOf, delexicalise; since 0.73. Works on: worlds with kinds (kind, class, is, is_a) and facts with keys.

A failing case is expanded into its related cases: perturbations along named axes, as in metamorphic testing (up the kinds, then down to other members), valid ones only. A fix is then judged on the case and on its related cases by distance, which measures how general it is. learnRules induces reading rules from cases (the library's induce, fillers lifted to their kind) and admits them only on held-out cases and related cases. learnFormOperators extracts the few operators of form (wrappers, fillers, passive) from aligned pairs and counts how many cover 80 % and 95 % of them. Each generates related cases and is a normalisation rule. Limits: the axes are fillers, keys and wording (operators, alternations, published paraphrases). Act families and world structure kinds come with the items of Q54. A filler related case's wording is the case's text with the filler replaced.

Read a language by its frames

In readFrames, senseOf; since 0.115. Works on: a language world with frames, parts, closed classes and std.morph morphology (w.lang.ro), and its lemma dictionary.

A request in another language is read straight into the act and roles of the reference language: "deseneaza doua case" is make, amount 2, thing house. The parts are matched in order, verbs by the act of their lemma's sense, nouns and adjectives through the morphology and the dictionary, so forms typed without diacritics read too. Limits: a word missing from the language's dictionary stays as typed; lists ("a house and three clouds") are one role. On PSys2's 161 real Romanian turns glossed by hand (node tools/bench-ro-frames.mjs): 65 % read by a frame; of the 121 glosses with an act, 75 % read with the right act and 68 % with the right act and thing (40 glosses have no verb to compare).

Read, revise, plan and say a request from what a world declares

In readRequest, requestWires, reviseRequest, planRequest, runPlan, capabilitiesOf, canBe, declares, honoursKind, joinedKinds, sayRequest, ASSUMED_SAY, ASSUMED_MAX, REQUEST_SOURCES, REQUEST_KEYS, VALUE_TYPES; since 0.149. Works on: the published w.request, w.dialogue (its revision facts), k.expect and the w.lang.en cues; any live world that declares std.schema.honours.

What was asked is a world: "draw a sketch, not circles" is a w.request with act make, target picture and a constraint kind circle not wanted; "5-10 nodes", "a few", "about 7", "a small pond", "in a corner", "with water plants and a duck", "about bees", "of 3 chapters" are constraints; every other content word is unread. A reaction ("too many", "no", "again", "put it back", "not all circles, better layout") revises the last request by the w.dialogue revision facts, and the result replaces the last one. A live world plans the request from what it declares (std.schema.honours act=make on=kind on=count ... of=picture, its kinds, its slots, its own k.expect facts), fills each gap from the sources in order (said > dialogue > induced > world > the catalog's k.expect > sampled; random varies within the typical range, never the minimum) and names what it assumed and what it does not honour. A new world gets this by declaring its capabilities and nothing else: examples/request-app.mjs runs two invented worlds (shapes on a page, short notes) through one session with no code per world, and test/request.test.mjs runs the properties P1-P8 over each, reading what it declares.

Bind a live world to its tool by wires, and run its plans with a timeline

In runPlan, runWorldPlan, toolBindings, toolTimeline, toolSession, toolCapabilities, askWorld, nearestAccepted, readView, stateDiff, mirrorState, stateIds, TOOL_ARG_SOURCES, WORLD_QUESTIONS, STATE_FEW, kindLineage, expectedValue, cutToCount, fillToCount, notAllOneKind, EXPECT_TYPES; since 0.152. Works on: any live world that declares std.tool.op / std.tool.param / std.tool.bind (and std.tool.custom), the published k.expect.scale, w.lang.en cues and replies.

The link between a live world and the generic mechanisms is wires: a world declares its tool's operations with their typed parameters and accepted values, binds each act of a plan to one of them with the source of every argument, and names its custom constraints and searches. The library fills the arguments (said, revised, the plan's values, conditional expectations k.expect.when), translates what the tool does not take by knowledge (the nearest grade of k.expect.scale, the nearest kind of the lineage or what std.schema.drawn says), checks preconditions, calls the primitive, reads the view before and after and says the diff ("I added 2 atlases", "Nothing changed. That is what is there already."). It keeps the timeline: one plan is one undo step, a revision replaces it, a question is no step. A question about the world ("what can you do here?", "how many novels?") is answered from its declarations and its view. examples/tool-app.mjs declares two invented worlds (books on a shelf, beads on a necklace) by wires and a few primitives, and runs them through toolSession with no code per world; test/tool.test.mjs runs properties over both.

Since 0.153 (test/acts-on-things.test.mjs): a revision is said as its net change ("too many" → "I removed the novel.", not every book removed and added again; a place the tool computes is not said); "make the comic blue" and "make it green" change what is there, decided by reference over the view (a definite kind with things of it, the last result), while "make a red comic" makes one; "remove everything", "remove the shelf" and, in the sketch, "delete the drawing" act on the whole world; a move says its place ("I moved the atlas to the left."); kinds said together keep each its own count, size and values ("a tiny novel and a big atlas", "a novel and a comic": one of each); a plural kind with no number takes its count from the expectations (k.expect.value of=book on=count when.plural=yes); a whole of the expectations whose parts the world cannot make is made as one thing (plan.asOne).

Since 0.154 (test/references-fragments.test.mjs, stateIds): the things of an act are the things of the state, never declaration rows ("remove everything" leaves every std.schema.drawn row); a thing's name or label is the object of any act ("move summary to the left", "connect start to end"), with the question when two things share it; a message with no verb that wants a kind ("a sun" after "draw a fish") continues the last act as a new request, never when it is a reaction or a question; "remove the atlases" takes every atlas, "remove the atlas" with two asks which; a turn read only in part never revises or keeps the last result; "bigger" steps the size and keeps the count; a revision that changes no key it set says "Nothing changed."; "how many beads?" counts the beads only; when.in=none in an expectation; std.tool.value gives a tool its own name for a value of the world; "draw a boat" says one boat as said, and the reply names an assumption by its source (ASSUMED_SAY, data), not a tool parameter's default.

Reason a little on the state of a live world before acting

In planRequest (its reasoning cycle), factsOf, kindFactsOf, deduceState, induceStyle, induceCorrections, REASONING, KIND_FACTS, REASON_RULES; since 0.150. Works on: any live world (its schema gives the view); the catalog's k.commonsense, k.scenes and k.kinds parents for what a kind expects.

The gap filler reasons a little, shallow and bounded, on the state of the concrete world, the same for every world. factsOf(world) is one flat view of any world as (thing, attribute, value) and (thing, relation, thing), kept per version and built from the last view by the changes since (also on a fork). planRequest proposes at most five candidates (where the things could go, a reference word such as "there"), runs each on a fork (generate-and-test), deduces on the simulated state by forward chaining to depth 2 (what the world refuses, a relation to nothing, the request's own bounds are hard; what the catalog expects of the kinds present is soft: a fish is at water, and a pond is a thing made of water), and keeps the cheapest survivor by weighted abduction (Hobbs et al. 1993: the assumptions weighted by their source; the shorter chain of inference is cheaper). Before that, one step of anti-unification (Plotkin 1970) over the world's things of a kind and over the session's corrections gives soft defaults that rank before the world's own facts and k.expect: after three blue circles a fourth is blue unless told otherwise; "too many" twice makes the next picture smaller. The plan says what it assumed, what it rejected and why, and what it cannot do. examples/request-app.mjs adds an invented garden (animals in areas, a pond with room for one fish): "add a fish" goes to the pond with room, "add a bird" to the tree, "add 9 fish in the pond" cannot be done and says why; test/request.test.mjs runs P9-P11 over every example world by its declarations.

Repair a near-miss word by the words of the live worlds

In repairWords, editCost, worldWords, languageWords, SPELL, SPELL_SOURCES, SPELL_COSTS, and readRequest with spell; since 0.151. Works on: any live world (its facts, kinds, words and actions), the dialogue's words, the language world's words.

A typo is repaired from context, not by a global dictionary alone: the open world's words rank first, then the other live worlds', then the dialogue's, then the language's (the order is data, SPELL_SOURCES). The cost is a keyboard-aware edit distance; a word is changed only on a unique best within the threshold, otherwise asks lists candidates.

Run an application's worlds with the host kit

In loadWorldSet, readTurn, turnScope, openSession, replyFrom, referentOf, countClass, clauses, cutPoints, chooseRewrite, snapshotSet, restoreSet, whyTurn, KIT_REPLIES; since 0.130. Works on: published world schemas (w.list, w.tasks, ...), a language world's frames, the knowledge the worlds join.

An application loads its worlds and reads each message: "make a shopping list", "add 2 apples to my shopping list" act; "make a purple dragon" is not understood and the reply names "purple" and "dragon"; "I have two kids" is a statement, not a request. Each world runs in its own World (w.tasks reads the w.list it imports), and a world with no kind of its own is refused. Every role a frame fills is used or named: "add a guest called Ann at table 1" on w.seating says "A guest has no place at a table." instead of naming her "ann at table 1". Only complete readings act, and a turn changes only the world it addresses. A session keeps the open question: "make a list" asks "What name?" and "groceries" names it; "There is no party list yet. Shall I make it?" is answered by yes; undo and redo step the world back and forward. Replies come from the language world's reply facts, in English or Romanian ("Am adăugat o casă."), with the plural class of the number by its CLDR rules. "add a table called one and add a table called two" is two requests; "remove the second table" and "remove it" name things; a host plugs in its own reader and takes navigation through a hook. "why?" answers from the last turn's facts ("The shape is round by default."), and a session saved as data comes back with its undo and redo. examples/lists-app.mjs is a whole application in a page. Limits: replies use the library's English act replies (a language world's reply templates are a knowledge ask); the binding of a frame's needs to a world's entry keys is given by the host; the session (pending questions, timeline) is the next part of the kit.

Step round a cycle, read a clock, say what sets a kind apart

In cycleOf, cycleAdd, cycleDiff, statedDay, readClock, writeClock, expandRecurrence, contrastOf; since 0.128. Works on: k.time's ordered members (weekdays, months, seasons, parts of the day), any kinds with parents (k.shapes).

"Today is Friday", then "what day is tomorrow" (Saturday); "how many days until Friday"; "3 o'clock plus 2 hours" (5 o'clock); "how is a circle different from other shapes" (no corners and no sides, round). Cycles are arithmetic over the knowledge's members, not a calendar in code. Limits: no dates and no time zones; the clock words are English until the language world has them.

Learn about the user from what they say

In readStatement, tellStatement, describeThing, readAboutSelf; since 0.127. Works on: a person type's keys and their words (w.person when published), a live world's session.

"I have two kids", "I am 40", "I like cats, dogs and horses", "my name is Ana" become facts on the user's thing, read by the keys' types; a list grows, a single value is replaced. "How many kids do I have" reads back through the same keys, and "what do you know about me" describes the thing. Only statements are read so: a request is not. Limits: one key per sentence; a statement whose key the person type has no word for is said, not guessed ("I am happy" without a mood key).

Sum a list: total, change, what is left

In listSums, listMeant; since 0.126. Works on: a list world (w.list) and an application's prices.

A shopping list's total (quantity × price per entry), the change from the money handed over, what is short, what is left of a budget, and whether it can be bought, all computed when asked and never stored. A request that names no list means the one made last. Real turns: "I want a puzzle and I pay 10 lei. What is the change?", "I want 2 pencils and I give the cashier 10 lei". Limits: prices are the application's (no knowledge world holds prices); an entry with no price leaves the total unknown, never guessed.

Build the typed world of a world

In typedWorldFor, schemaOf, fitsSlot, freeTextType; since 0.114. Works on: a world's schema (a World, or a host's description of its kinds and keys) and the knowledge its slots join.

The typed readers need typed facts. A world made of patterns and a matrix gets them from its schema and the knowledge it joins: kinds and keys with their words, closed sets, the joined things, the session's things, and a type for every free-text slot. A slot then refuses words that do not fit it, such as a relation read as an instrument's name. Limits: a free-text slot's type is guessed from its key unless the host names it; a join must be named by the schema or the host.

Compose a diagram from knowledge

In composeDiagram, sampleDiagram, diagramTopics, diagramKinds, arrangementOf; since 0.112. Works on: k.diagrams (kinds, fills, shapes) and the knowledge its fills name; w.diagram for the result.

A diagram kind × a topic whose knowledge has the kind's structure: a cycle of the seasons from their next= facts, a tree of kinds from is_a=, a mind map of a bee from its parts, a Venn diagram of the kinds under a kind, a network of a country's neighbours. The nodes, edges and groups are w.diagram wires, shaped by k.diagrams shapes and laid out by the kind's layout with arrange. An arrangement request ("in a circle", "from right to left") is read by the language world's phrases and changes the layout. sampleDiagram draws a random kind × topic. Limits: a topic with no knowledge of the kind's structure gets a reason, not a diagram (a flowchart of making tea needs a procedure for it). A tree goes down breadth first, up to max nodes.

Align two worlds by structure

In alignWorlds; since 0.77. Works on: any two world schemas.

A seating world and a timeline world align: table to day, seat to slot, guest to event. Types are paired by the shape of their slots and references, not by their names. What one has and the other lacks (a default, a closed set of choices, a required slot) is offered with the map, never told. Limits: the offers are defaults, choices and required slots. Rules, procedures and question forms come next. The pairing is greedy (the best pairs first).

Measure what a mechanism buys

In experiment, ablation; since 0.96. Works on: any host function and a set of cells with expectations.

Cells with expectations become ledger rows. An ablation runs every configuration on every set and reports a table, the cells each mechanism gains or loses against the baseline, and the failures grouped by cause. Limits: the host's run and judge decide what a pass is. The rows are in memory: a host appends them to its ledger with ledgerAppend.

Learn worlds from use (agent skills)

In skills/; since 0.97. Works on: an application, its failures and held-out rows, and the knowledge repositories in repos.pin.

Eight skills turn the library's tools into steps an agent follows, always generically. A failing turn is triaged by its request evidence to the generic piece it needs (a declaration, a knowledge fact, a language word, a composition, a method, a library mechanism). The piece is built, or the turn stays on the honest list as "needs <generic piece>". The skills are: improve worlds from failures, propose a generic method (with the patch-smell checklist), author a live world, learn a world from examples, learn language rules, audit knowledge, expand and test a case, and consolidate. The gates are properties generated from the declared worlds, fresh probes and the honest list shrinking, with a two-phase acceptance rule. Each skill has a dry run, listed in skills/README.md. Limits: the dry runs read psys2's form pairs and the pinned catalog. A host's own run needs its adapter: the reader and the answers.
