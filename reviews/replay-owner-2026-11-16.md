## 2. The gaps, with a proposal for each

Each proposal is stated at the general level, with the four areas in mind, not for the sentence that revealed it.

| Gap | What is missing, and what I propose |
|---|---|
| A. Language at the edges (turns 1–13, 26–30, 33–42) | The book has no section on language. The owner's rule is fixed (the selector decides; browser translation when available, then the on-device model, then rules; chosen by reading). The book must say where translation sits among the stages of 6.2, what is kept untranslated (names the user gave, quoted text), what happens to a word the translator does not know, and that the reply is translated back. For errors: a correction may span a word boundary ("th epond"); a non-standard form of a known word ("upper") is read by its stem when the standard reading gives no request. Each such repair is said once in the reply. |
| B. Referring to a thing by a neighbouring kind (turns 11, 12, 13, 17, 19, 20, 26–29) | Ten turns. The user says "lake", "pond", "water" for the one blue area. Proposal, three rules. (1) If no thing of the said kind exists, the candidates are the existing things whose kind is nearest in the kind tree (ancestor, descendant or sibling under a close common ancestor), within a declared distance; one candidate → take it and say so once ("I took the water as the lake"); several → ask. (2) The word the user used becomes a name of that thing in this instance (an alias, with provenance), so the next "the lake" resolves at once. (3) If the said kind is more specific than the thing's kind and compatible with it, the thing's kind may be narrowed to it (the water IS a lake now), which lets knowledge about lakes apply. The same rules serve "the box" for a node, "the paragraph" for a section, "the call" for a meeting. |
| C. Things that fill an area, and what is in front of what (turns 1, 2, 18, 30, 31) | Water, ground, sky, grass, a patch: their natural description is the region they cover, not a point and a size. Proposal: a second form of place for a thing, "covers a region" (the region is the value; size is derived), declared on the kind in knowledge (kind of extent: point-like, linear, area). And a third axis of the page, the layer, with the relations "in front of" and "behind": needed as soon as two drawn things overlap, and needed to give "over" its covering sense. Both are general: a background band in a diagram, a highlighted range in a document, an all-day event in the calendar are area-like things too. |
| D. Messages about the conversation itself (turns 13, 20, 38) | Three kinds are missing from 6.4. An objection ("how come?", "that is not a real child"): disagreement with the last reply or result. A report about the state ("the lake is not there"): the user says what he sees. A plain "why?" exists already. Proposal: for a report, read the state again, compare it with what the last reply claimed, and either correct the state or correct the claim, saying which. For an objection to a refusal, answer with the explanation of 6.11 AND the nearest thing that could have been meant (which is rule B1). For an objection to a result, treat it as a reaction on the quality it names (for turn 38, the manner of drawing, gap G). |
| E. "Something else" (turns 5, 6) | Proposal: a reaction "other" = redo the corrected request with one more constraint: the new result must be at least a declared distance away from the previous one, measured in the spaces of the roles the system chose (kind, colours, places). It replaces the step, like any reaction. It is the opposite of "the same again" and uses the distance of 4.4.1; nothing new is needed except the constraint. |
| F. What "better arranged" measures (turns 8, 9) | Proposal: every instance has a declared arrangement cost: the weighted sum of its soft issues about place (a fish out of water, a house on the sky), of overlaps between things that should not overlap, and of crowding (distance below a minimum). "Arrange better" = search for places that lower the cost, changing only places, keeping everything else (R36). "More naturally" = the same with the weight on the knowledge issues. The reply says what was moved and why, from the issues that disappeared. The diagram's "nicer" and the calendar's "tidy up my Friday" are the same operation with their own cost terms. |
| G. How a thing is drawn when there is no picture, and how well (turns 33, 34, 38, 39, 40) | Three additions. (1) The order of fallbacks for a realisation: own picture; the picture of the nearest ancestor; composition from the kind's parts (knowledge: a swing has a frame, ropes and a seat) using basic shapes; only then "I cannot draw that". (2) What distinguishes a kind from the ancestor whose picture is borrowed must be applied when the tool can (a grandfather: grey hair; a little girl: smaller) and said when it cannot. This needs the differences stated in knowledge as qualities of the kind. (3) The manner of drawing is a quality with a space (from schematic to realistic); "not a skeleton, a real child" is a step on it, and the tool declares which part of that space it can realise. |
| H. Where on a thing another thing goes (turns 35, 37) | Proposal: in knowledge, a kind can name the part that supports, contains or attaches: a swing supports on its seat, a table on its top, a pond contains in its water. A relation with such a thing is resolved to that part before the geometric test. Without it, "on the swing" is on top of the frame. The same in a document ("in the letter" → the body, not the header). |
| I. Becoming something else; requests addressed to the assistant (turns 42–51) | Ten turns. "Replace" (4.6.1) does not do this: it makes a new thing. (2) The assistant has a face in the drawing; "you", an imperative with no object and a verb of state ("become", "look", "smile") refer to it. The book needs the addressee as a referent, declared once (the role, not the word). |
| J. Choosing the kind and the structure (turns 3, 4) | Completion must cover the kind (what is usual on this kind of page, given what is already there) and the structure of a whole (which optional parts, how many), with the same sources and the same "say what was assumed". |
| K. A fragment that keeps the act and changes the object (turn 26) | One sentence in 6.4: a fragment may replace any role of the previous request, the object included; the act is inherited. |
| L. Which sense of a relation word (turn 18) | Each relation word lists its senses in the language world; the choice uses what knowledge says about the two kinds (a house does not float; a bridge goes across), then the default sense; if the senses change the world differently and nothing chooses, ask (6.3.1). |

## 3. What this says about the book

The book is strong on values and weak on things. Everything about a value (a colour, a size, an hour) is now precise: spaces, distance, degree, tolerance, learning. The owner's session fails elsewhere: on WHICH thing he means (B), on what kind of thing it is and how it occupies the page (C), on how it is drawn (G), on where exactly on it something goes (H), on turning it into something else (I), and on talking about what just happened (D). None of these is in the three toy sentences the book keeps using.
