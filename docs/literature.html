The library and the literature

One line per concept: what the library does, the established term and reference, and where the library differs. SOP Lang's own vocabulary is in the glossary, with its standard equivalents.

LibraryEstablished term and referenceDifference
relatedCases, judgeOnRelated, generalisationTestmetamorphic testing (Chen, Cheung and Yiu 1998); behavioural testing with invariance and directional tests, CheckList (Ribeiro et al. 2020); contrast sets (Gardner et al. 2020)perturbations come from the world's graph of kinds and the language world; the world computes the expected answer (docs/generalisation.md)
delexicalisedelexicalisation in dialogue state tracking and NLG (Henderson, Thomson and Young 2014; Wen et al. 2015)values are replaced by their types from the world's typed lexicon, not by slot names of a fixed ontology
resolveEllipsisellipsis and fragment (non-sentential utterance) resolution (Fernández, Ginzburg and Lappin 2007)only verbless continuations of the previous act, with roles read by type
paraphrasesOf, realiseFrameparaphrase generation (Madnani and Dorr 2010)paraphrases are declared by the language world as frame pairs, not learnt from corpora
learnRankinglearning to rank (Liu 2009)a search over the order of a few declared criteria (a lexicographic ranking), not a learnt scoring function
calibrateprobability calibration: Platt scaling (Platt 1999), isotonic regression (Zadrozny and Elkan 2002)maps a reader's confidence (for example readEnsemble's agreement) to a probability, fit on held-out rows
std.planhierarchical task network planning: methods, tasks, decomposition; SHOP2 (Nau et al. 2003)methods and choice points are world facts; choice points are ranked by std.decide
std.compose, summarize, realise, morphthe NLG pipeline (Reiter and Dale 2000): document planning (content determination, document structuring), microplanning (lexicalisation, aggregation, referring expressions), surface realisationcontent determination reads narration and interest facts, document structure comes from genres, lexicalisation from the language world's templates and phrasings
alignWorldsstructure mapping (Gentner 1983); the Structure-Mapping Engine (Falkenhainer, Forbus and Gentner 1989)two world schemas are aligned by the signatures of their slots, and pairings whose references map to paired types score more (a form of systematicity); what one has and the other lacks is offered, never told
std.decide costsweighted abduction, interpretation as abduction (Hobbs, Stickel, Appelt and Martin 1993)criteria are compared in order (lexicographically), not summed as one cost
tiersevidence and provenance levels; default reasoning (Reiter 1980); truth maintenance (Doyle 1979)every value carries a rank of how it got there (told, derived, forced, default, guessed); a derived value takes the weakest tier of its sources; ranks, not probabilities
induce, learnRulesanti-unification, least general generalisation (Plotkin 1970); rule induction in inductive logic programming (Muggleton 1991)generalisation lifts fillers to their kind in the world before anti-unifying, and rules are admitted on held-out and related cases
signatureOf, chunk, minePatternssupertagging (Bangalore and Joshi 1999); word classes (Brown et al. 1992); cascaded finite-state chunking (Abney 1996; FASTUS, Hobbs et al. 1997)signatures are types from the world's lexicon; patterns are mined from a corpus and counted for coverage
readEnsemble, riskCoverageensembles with reranking (Charniak and Johnson 2005); selective prediction and risk-coverage curves (Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017)the readers' agreement is the confidence, ranked by std.decide criteria rather than a trained reranker
interestOfthe appraisal model of interest (Silvia 2005, 2006): novelty-complexity and coping potentialthe appraisals, features and weight ranges are a knowledge world (k.interest), measured on the facts of a world
planRequest (its reasoning cycle), factsOf, deduceState, induceStylegenerate-and-test with constraints; forward chaining, bottom-up evaluation as in Datalog (Ceri, Gottlob and Tanca 1989); weighted abduction (Hobbs, Stickel, Appelt and Martin 1993); anti-unification (Plotkin 1970)at most five candidates, each run on a fork; chaining stops at depth 2 and under a tick budget; the abductive costs are the places of the sources in a declared order, compared lexicographically by std.decide; generalisation is one step, over the things of one kind or the session's corrections
std.motionrule-based scheduling of nonverbal behaviour, such as blinks and visemes (the Behavior Markup Language, Kopp et al. 2006)schedules are world rules over time, not an animation timeline format
