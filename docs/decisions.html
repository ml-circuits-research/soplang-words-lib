How the library decides

Every place where the library picks one of several candidates is a choice point. The owner's question (17:16) was whether one method decides them all. The answer the library works toward is yes:

  • each choice point produces candidates;
  • choose(world, kind, candidates) orders them by the criteria declared for its kind, as data (std.decide.criterion of=<kind> n= name= measure= dir=hard|min|max);
  • every loss is explained by the criterion it lost on.

The order of the criteria

The order is the same for every kind; a kind lists only the criteria that apply to it.

  1. Hard constraints. The candidate is consistent with the world and the types fit (dir=hard: anything but 0 loses).
  2. Coverage. Nothing said is left unread, and every role is filled.
  3. The fewest and cheapest assumptions (weighted abduction). Each default, near word, form operator or guessed value has a cost. The costs are knowledge: word senses give 1 / 2 / 3, and tiers give told < derived < forced < default < guessed.
  4. Specificity. The more specific rule, frame or template beats the general one.
  5. Priors. Typicality, interest (k.interest), recency and focus.
  6. A seeded order. The same seed gives the same choice.

calibrate(world, kind, cases) learns a kind's order from cases and proposes it as facts, admitted only by held-out cases. A world, or a knowledge world, may declare criteria for a kind of its own. For a kind std.decide already declares, a host gives its list directly with criteria. Costs and priors should come from k.* facts, and tie-break weights may be calibrated on the case corpus, admitted by held-out cases, never tuned in code.

Two kinds of choice points differ on purpose:

  • Deciding (a reading, a template, a word, a rule) takes the best candidate by the criteria above.
  • Drawing (sampling, story elements, composition variety) takes a weighted random candidate among those that hold the hard constraints, because variety is the point.

Both use the same hard constraints, and both use the same knowledge for costs and priors.

Explaining a choice

choose returns ranked: for each candidate its place and, for the losers, lostTo and on (the criterion) with a one-line why. For example, "cost 2; #1: cost 1": this word was a kind away, and the winner a synonym.
